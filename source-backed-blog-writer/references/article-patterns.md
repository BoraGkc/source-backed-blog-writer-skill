# Article pattern library

Choose one primary pattern from the reader's job and observed search intent. Combine patterns only when the reader genuinely has two jobs; name the primary pattern and the adaptation.

## Selection map

| Reader job | Primary pattern |
|---|---|
| Replace a named product | Alternatives |
| Choose within a product category | Best-Of |
| Decide between two named products | Comparison |
| Complete a task where a product is relevant | Product-Led How-To |
| Complete a task without a necessary product tie-in | Editorial How-To |
| Browse examples, tips, ideas, resources, or statistics | List |
| Understand a term or concept | What-Is |
| Understand a current event and its implications | Newsjacking |
| Learn a broad topic from one authoritative page | Pillar |
| Interpret a central visual asset | Infographic |

Treat commercial investigation as a decision task even when the query looks informational. Let the observed results and reader need determine the pattern.

## Commercial patterns

### Alternatives

**Reader outcome:** identify credible replacements for a named product.

**Structure:** open with the concrete reason someone may switch; give each option a consistent decision-oriented section covering fit, relevant benefits, tradeoffs, current pricing when verified, and onboarding. End with a comparison table and recommendations by use case.

**Proof standard:** explain the criteria, distinguish verified facts from analysis, and never generalize from a single complaint.

**CTA:** present the user's product proportionately and disclose the relationship.

### Best-Of

**Reader outcome:** choose the strongest option for a specific need.

**Structure:** state the inclusion method, provide a scannable shortlist, then give each option its own section with best-for label, evidence, benefits, limitations, and decision-relevant pricing. Follow with selection criteria, a compact comparison, and recommendations by use case.

**Proof standard:** use the same criteria across options. Do not invent measurements, scores, or firsthand testing.

**CTA:** place the user's product early only when it earns that position and disclose the relationship.

### Comparison

**Reader outcome:** choose between two named products.

**Structure:** give an immediate decision summary and fact-checked table; introduce each product with the same categories; compare important shared criteria under identical conditions; finish with who should choose each.

**Proof standard:** verify features and prices immediately before drafting. State unknowns and avoid selective criteria.

**CTA:** recommend by fit, not by unsupported claims of overall superiority.

### Product-Led How-To

**Reader outcome:** complete a task and understand where the product helps.

**Structure:** define the result and prerequisites, show a numbered overview, then give each step its own section with action, expected result, and likely failure mode. Add the product only at the step where it changes the work.

**Proof standard:** use screenshots, demonstrations, or internal expertise where available. Do not claim the product is necessary when a native or simpler method works.

**CTA:** connect the product to the demonstrated task and omit benefits the article has not established.

## Editorial patterns

### Editorial How-To

**Reader outcome:** complete a task accurately.

**Structure:** define audience, outcome, and scope; list the steps; expand each step with prerequisites, exact action, expected result, example, and troubleshooting. Add tips only when they contribute new information.

**Proof standard:** show calculations and cite consequential claims.

### List

**Reader outcome:** scan useful options or examples organized by a clear principle.

**Structure:** state the inclusion rule, then give every item a reason to belong, intended user, evidence, and tradeoff. Group items only when categories improve navigation.

**Proof standard:** do not pad the count or imply exhaustive coverage without evidence.

### What-Is

**Reader outcome:** understand a concept and why it matters.

**Structure:** give a direct definition immediately, explain its practical effect, then add the most useful expansion: a worked calculation, real examples, or actionable guidance.

**Proof standard:** distinguish a standard definition from the author's synthesis.

### Newsjacking

**Reader outcome:** understand a recent event and its implications.

**Structure:** timestamp the article; lead with confirmed facts and the original source; explain necessary context; separate current facts from analysis; narrow implications and actions to the site's actual audience.

**Proof standard:** prioritize official or original reporting, label uncertainty, and add an update note while the story is developing.

**Boundary:** do not exploit tragedy, publish rumors as fact, or add company opinions without authorization.

### Pillar

**Reader outcome:** get a broad, navigable overview.

**Structure:** define the audience and coverage, add a table of contents, then select only the necessary sections from definition, evolution, key terms, benefits and drawbacks, examples, process, evaluation, and further resources. Link to verified cluster content and identify genuine cluster gaps.

**Proof standard:** favor information density over length and keep every section independently useful.

### Infographic

**Reader outcome:** understand a visual and retain its essential information without relying on sight alone.

**Structure:** explain what the visual shows and why it matters, place the infographic, reproduce its essential facts in accessible text, and add only context the image cannot carry.

**Proof standard:** cite the underlying data and write alt text for the visual's purpose, not for keyword coverage.

**Boundary:** use this pattern only when an infographic exists or will actually be produced.

## Shared rules

- Put the decisive answer or fact at the start of each section.
- Delete optional sections that do not advance the reader's job.
- Use tables only when comparison becomes easier to scan.
- Keep evaluation criteria consistent across competing products.
- Disclose material relationships and separate facts, firsthand evidence, and analysis.
- Treat FAQs as reader service, not a promise of rich results.
- Describe an image's useful information or function in its alt text; use empty alt text for purely decorative images.
